📊 Reading the table

  • Rank — suggested confidence order: top row = your 16, bottom = your 1.
  • Favorites are highlighted blue.
  • The recommended side is highlighted blue — the favorite, or the underdog when the moneyline implies the favorite is weaker than the spread suggests (those rows read (dog)).
  • Spread — the consensus point spread, shown from the home team's view: negative means the home team is favored (−7 = home favored by 7), positive means the away team is favored (+7 = away favored by 7).
  • Win % — chance the favorite wins outright (the Model + Market blend).
  • Cover % — chance the recommended side beats the spread (where the moneyline and spread disagree). Games without moneylines read ~50% (no signal).
  • Total — projected combined score (average over/under across books).
  • Tiebreaker — per league rules it's the last kickoff of the week; its blue Total is the combined score to guess.
  • Games run Tuesday-to-Tuesday, matching standard pick 'em weeks.

💹 Market (moneylines)

A moneyline is a pure "who wins" price. Decimal odds convert straight to probability: 1 ÷ 1.56 ≈ 64%. Books shade both sides ~4–5% as their commission (the vig), so we normalize them back to a fair 100% and average every book that posted a line. This is Vegas' collective opinion of win probability — including subtleties like NFL's key numbers of 3 and 7.

In ATS mode this is the engine: Cover % is built from the de-vigged moneyline, then compared against the posted spread to find which side beats the line.

📐 Model (spread + total)

Our independent estimate assumes final margins follow a bell curve centered on the average spread. Game randomness is ~13.5 points of standard deviation in an average scoring environment (44-point total) and grows with shootouts — so −7 in a 38-total game wins ~71% but only ~68% at a 56 total. Same spread, different risk.

In ATS mode the model can't call a cover on its own — it's derived from the line itself — so it only fills in when a game has no moneyline, and those games correctly read ~50% (no signal).

🎚️ The slider

Sets how much Market (sportsbook moneylines) vs Model (spread + total math) feeds the Win % column (default 50/50, saved in this browser). Games without posted moneylines always fall back to the model alone. When Market and Model disagree by a lot, the gap itself is worth a look before locking picks.

🏈 League formats

Straight-up ranks by each favorite's chance of winning outright. ATS ("against the spread") ranks by each side's chance of beating the line — the favorite only pays off if it wins by more than the posted spread. Because the spread already reflects the teams' strength, the ATS pick is driven by where the moneyline and the spread disagree: an implied-strength check that sometimes flips the recommendation to the underdog (marked dog). Games without moneylines read ~50% (no signal) and sort by their straight-up odds as a tiebreak. The Monday-night total tiebreaker is unchanged in both modes.

⚖️ Sportsbook weighting

When averaging lines across sportsbooks we don't treat every book equally. Regulated "sharp" books (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM) get double weight — their lines move fast on sharp money. Offshore "soft" books (BetUS, MyBookie.ag, Bovada, LowVig, BetOnline) get half weight — they update slowly and carry wide vig with public bias. Any other book is neutral (×1). This keeps a stale soft line from dragging the consensus at equal weight to a fresh, sharp number.
